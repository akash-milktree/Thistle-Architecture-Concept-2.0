## 1. Claremont Road, Luton  (`claremont-road-luton`)

July 2026. Site already says "Three-Bed House To Seven-Bed HMO", permitted
development then full planning, Go. All confirmed.

- **Bedrooms** 7, all en suite, in two stages
- **Room sizes** No room below 10 sqm, against a 6.51 sqm legal minimum
- **Communal space** About 32 sqm, against a 23.5 sqm target for seven persons
- **Planning route** Six-bed C4 plus loft dormer is permitted development under an
  LDC; the seventh bedroom is sui generis and needs full planning
- **Key risk** Protection of family housing, applied through Luton Policies LLP1
  and LLP15, which is why the scheme is phased
- **Indicative** Gross rent about £56,700 a year on seven rooms

**The Brief.** Could a large, already-extended mid-terrace take a seven-bedroom
all-en-suite HMO, and what route gets there with least risk?

**What We Found.** Six bedrooms plus a rear dormer loft are permitted
development. The seventh is sui generis and turns on family-housing protection,
so the scheme is phased: establish and tenant the six-bed C4 first, and the
seventh is then judged as an addition to an existing HMO rather than the loss of
a family home. A nearby approval at 39 Hazelbury Crescent granted a seventh
person on exactly that basis. The 20 sqm outbuilding becomes shared co-working
space, which officers view favourably.

**Our Recommendation.** Proceed to seven bedrooms in two stages. Take the six-bed
LDC first and implement it properly, with tenancy and licence records kept, then
apply for the seventh. Eight persons is possible later but not recommended as a
base case.

**Documents** Feasibility Overview 4pp · Planning Research 7pp · Space Standards
3pp. Luton Borough Council HMO Standards, 33pp, is the guidance link.

**Roadmap** Measured survey → Six-bed LDC → Implement and tenant → Full planning
for the seventh → Technical design → Construction

## 2. Hathaway Road, Croydon  (`hathaway-road-croydon`)

July 2026. Site already says "When The HMO Is The Wrong Answer", C3(b) supported
living, **No-Go**. Confirmed, and this is the most interesting of the five.

- **Bedrooms** 5, sizes 11 to 18 sqm
- **Communal space** 16 sqm
- **Planning route** Not an HMO. C3(b) supported living, confirmed by LDC
- **Key risk** Finding a care or support provider willing to operate it
- **Constraint** Gross internal floor area is 93 to 115 sqm, below Croydon's
  130 sqm threshold

**The Brief.** Could this house become an HMO?

**What We Found.** No. Croydon protects family homes below 130 sqm from
conversion to an HMO under Policies SP2 and DM1.2, and this property is below
that threshold, so an HMO application would be very likely to be refused. A
different use answers it: C3(b) supported living needs no HMO licence, and the
building suits it, with five rooms of 11 to 18 sqm and 16 sqm of communal space.

**Our Recommendation.** Do not pursue an HMO. Identify and engage a care or
support provider willing to operate the property as C3(b) before committing to
works or purchase, confirm their requirements on room count and communal space,
verify the floor area by measured survey, and speak to a specialist broker on end
value.

**Documents** Feasibility Overview 4pp · Planning Research 9pp. Croydon HMO
Standards, 8pp, is the guidance link.

**Roadmap** Engage a support provider → Confirm provider requirements → Measured
survey → Broker on end value → C3(b) LDC

> Worth flagging: this is the one No-Go in the set and it argues the value of a
> feasibility better than any of the others. It is a good candidate to lead with.

## 3. Bath Street, Cheddar  (`bath-street-cheddar`)

June 2026. Site already says "Former Bank To Eleven-Bed HMO And Shop", full
planning sui generis mixed use, Go. Confirmed.

- **Bedrooms** 11, all en suite, 10 sqm smallest to 22 sqm largest
- **Communal space** 27.5 sqm kitchen and lounge on the first floor, plus a
  10 sqm kitchen on the mansard serving the upper cluster
- **Commercial** 45.2 sqm Class E unit retained at the Bath Street frontage
- **Planning route** Full planning, sui generis, mixed use
- **Key risk** Heritage harm to the setting of the Market Cross, and conflict
  with Policy EE1 of the Cheddar Neighbourhood Plan
- **Indicative** Shop modelled at roughly £12,000 to £15,000 a year gross

**The Brief.** Could a former Lloyds Bank in a village centre become an HMO
without losing the shop the neighbourhood plan protects?

**What We Found.** Yes, as a mixed use. The 45.2 sqm Class E unit stays at the
frontage, with an 11-bed sui generis HMO above and behind it across ground,
first, second and a new mansard. The principle of residential conversion is
already established by prior approval 17/25/00004 of March 2025, and the rear car
park removes the highways question.

**Our Recommendation.** Proceed as mixed use, retaining the shop. The two real
risks are heritage harm to the Market Cross setting and Policy EE1, both of which
are design and evidence matters rather than blockers.

**Documents** Feasibility Overview 6pp · Planning Research 10pp.

**Roadmap** Measured survey → Heritage statement → Pre-application → Full
planning → Technical design → Construction

## 4. Beech House Road, Croydon  (`beech-house-road-croydon`)

June 2026. Site already says "Six-Bed HMO To Thirteen", full planning sui
generis, Go. Confirmed.

- **Bedrooms** 13, all en suite, 12.6 to 27 sqm, against a Croydon en suite
  minimum of 12.5 sqm
- **Communal space** 26 sqm kitchen and 13.8 sqm dining on the ground floor, a
  second kitchen on the second floor, and a 9.1 sqm utility
- **Planning route** Full planning, sui generis
- **Key risk** Political rather than policy: the site is in a conservation area
  and ward councillors have opposed nearby HMO schemes
- **Indicative** Gross rent £11,700 a month, £140,400 a year

**The Brief.** Could an existing six-bed C4 HMO be taken to thirteen bedrooms?

**What We Found.** Yes, entirely within the existing envelope, through internal
alterations only. The lawful six-bed use granted under 24/04169/FUL in May 2025
is the baseline, so no family dwelling is lost. Croydon has no adopted HMO
density threshold and a directly comparable 13-bed sui generis HMO was approved
at 51 Addiscombe Road in 2022.

**Our Recommendation.** Proceed at thirteen bedrooms. The planning case is sound;
the risk to manage is political, given a 7-bed refusal at committee at 10
Woodstock Road in May 2025, so the application needs to be presented carefully.

**Documents** Feasibility Overview 5pp. A 15pp property pack also exists but is
not a client report.

**Roadmap** Measured survey → Design development → Pre-application →
Full planning → Technical design → Construction

## 5. St Johns, Aylesbury  (`st-johns-aylesbury`)

March 2026. Site already says "Nine-Bed HMO Conversion", full planning sui
generis, Go. Confirmed.

- **Bedrooms** 9, all en suite, 12 to 16 sqm
- **Communal space** About 25 sqm total
- **Planning route** Full planning, sui generis, with a six-bed permitted
  development fallback as the site is not in an Article 4 area
- **Key risk** Nine bedrooms sits at the upper end of what is likely to be
  supported, so overdevelopment is the concern

**The Brief.** How many bedrooms will this town-centre building actually support?

**What We Found.** Nine is achievable at feasibility stage, with generous rooms
of 12 to 16 sqm and about 25 sqm of amenity, which exceeds what recent officer
reports have required. Local precedent is strong for seven and eight bedroom
HMOs. There is also a six-bed permitted development fallback, subject to due
diligence, because the site is not in an Article 4 area.

**Our Recommendation.** Proceed at nine, with careful design to answer
overdevelopment concerns, and the six-bed permitted development route held as a
fallback.

**Documents** Feasibility Overview 7pp. No separate Planning Research or Space
Standards in the folder.

**Roadmap** Measured survey → Design development → Pre-application →
Full planning → Technical design → Construction
